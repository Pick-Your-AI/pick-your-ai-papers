# Charter

## Mission and audience

Build the research and delivery foundation for a maintainable, source-linked directory of AI scholarly work. Readers should be able to discover a work, identify the exact version behind an assertion, understand the limits of that assertion, trace its related artifacts, and see later corrections and independent scrutiny.

The intended audiences are researchers navigating adjacent fields, engineers assessing methods, students following research history, reviewers checking citations and conditions, and contributors maintaining the Pick Your AI reference corpus. A useful directory supports these questions without presenting citation popularity or paper inclusion as scientific endorsement.

The parent authorization is [research #509](https://github.com/Pick-Your-AI/pick-your-ai-research/issues/509). The current mission explicitly requests the papers repository and Project; earlier audit text that described it as a proposal is retained as history rather than treated as current execution authorization.

## Owned scope

The repository owns the literature-oriented research method, independent scholarly identity/schema proposals, citation provenance, authorship and organization references, methods and architectures, claim and experiment descriptions, reproduction assessments, correction events, historical relationships, coverage measurement, and receiving contracts for eligible scholarly releases.

It researches academic publications and preprints, plus separately typed technical reports, theses, surveys, position papers, methods descriptions, artifact records and correction notices where they support a declared literature scope. Venue and object labels are evidence fields; a prestigious venue or commercial laboratory does not bypass review.

## Boundaries

Existing T1 model selection, T5 training and T6 evaluation/deployment research remain authoritative for their technical decisions. Existing market research remains authoritative for market findings. The frozen 29-kind builder contract remains unchanged. Dataset directory records, application products, shared ingestion services and website implementation retain their separate owners; papers may reference them through versioned, accepted handoffs.

This package does not collect restricted full text, store private datasets, train models, run paid experiments, impersonate authors, create consent, or establish source-use rights. It does not promise a census of every historical or future AI work. “Near-exhaustive” is an objective pursued with transparent coverage strata, denominators and residual gaps.

## Inclusion and exclusion

Include a candidate when its relationship to a declared AI research domain can be explained, its identity is traceable to a primary scholarly or author/publisher record, and its source/material can be handled within a recorded permitted scope. Incomplete evidence can remain a clearly marked research note.

Exclude from admitted public output duplicate identities, unverifiable references, unsupported summaries, unrelated marketing pages presented as papers, private material and uses lacking a qualifying rights decision. Keep an exclusion reason and appropriate non-sensitive provenance so excluded candidates do not disappear from the coverage calculation. Retractions and negative results are not excluded solely because they are unfavorable; they require accurate status and context.

## Milestones and success criteria

The release phases are Foundation, Metadata Pilot, Evidence Pilot, Website Handoff and Maintained Index. Each phase needs a bounded acceptance packet; no phase succeeds through issue count alone.

Foundation establishes usable instructions, schemas, validation, the declared issue hierarchy and verified GitHub settings. Metadata Pilot demonstrates identity and source-check quality on a declared sample. Evidence Pilot adds reviewed claims and reproducibility distinctions without fabricating experiments. Website Handoff requires an eligible immutable release and a receiving consumer decision. Maintained Index requires operational ownership, correction processing and observable coverage/freshness work.

Measure duplicate/error rates, traceability, known unknowns, per-stratum coverage, review throughput, unresolved corrections and eligible release counts. Define each numerator and denominator before reporting a percentage. Scientific validity and completeness are not inferred from these operational measures.
